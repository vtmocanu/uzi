# PRD #3607: Compact policy-refusal hold

**Status:** Approved for dispatch (lead and buddy, after independent Opus and Astra architecture reviews). One new issue, one gated implementation run, one PR. Parent: #3068, now an umbrella. 

**Decision authority:** Owner-approved compact scope and attempt-path product choice, 2026-10-29. This replaces #3068's grant/delivery implementation, D15-D19 Stop override and former complete hold-v1 contract. For effective-attempt-path lanes, the owner chose consent once per refusal episode: after confirmed fresh-from-saved-work Resume, later claims behave as today until another refusal. For session-compatible lanes, the applied-refusal preserving constraint remains until terminal unless the run later enters an attempt path and the owner explicitly resolves that new fresh-from-saved-work episode. That resolution ends the earlier constraint for the remaining run until another refusal. It does not claim that an unapplied refusal is globally visible. Existing preservation, custody and unrelated security guarantees remain; ADR-2149 is unchanged.

**Review status:** P0 is resolved by the owner decision above. Both independent architecture reviews requested revisions, with no BLOCK; their findings and the product decision are folded here. Final reviewer and lead/buddy approval are still required before filing/dispatch.

## Problem

A Codex root turn classified cyberPolicy or misalignmentPolicyViolation currently ends its run as provider_policy_refusal. The owner can lose the opportunity to continue with narrower guidance even though files and a conversation may remain usable. Conversely, ordinary Codex recovery can fall back from a rejected/missing conversation to a fresh one. That fallback must not silently reset a conversation after a policy refusal.

The earlier #3068 design coupled this outcome to accepted-plan Stop authority, revision receipts and a broad implementation/proof program. Its preserved checkpoint is work in progress, not an implementation baseline or permission to ship those unfinished pieces.

## Outcome

Eligible **Codex, noninteractive, root** refusals on **issue, task, prompt and self_improve** runs enter an owner hold. Use status paused and the distinct hold_reason **codex_policy_hold**, following the existing typed provider-hold pattern. Keep terminal fail_origin provider_policy_refusal unchanged; the two literals must not be conflated. Root initial planning and implementation are covered. Revise-turn admission is conditional: a root refusal while awaiting_approval holds only if the narrow current-claim/current-gate proof below is delivered. Otherwise explicitly omit this extension, preserving today's terminal refusal with retained work for that path. Child, chat, interactive, Claude, judge, job, cross-check and other kinds keep their current behavior.

There is no timer-based retry of an unresolved policy episode. Preserve files, the original session identity for audit, custody and supported worker affinity. Clearly distinguish API-confirmed hold from locally pending delivery, and verified/published capture from local-only or unverified preservation. A policy classification is an observed provider result, not an explanation of why the provider refused.

**Guarantee boundary:** the durable server state protects applied refusals under the lane-specific consent rule. Surviving local pending evidence blocks new work on its own worker before application. If that worker/journal is absent past TerminalPendingLease (default one hour) and another worker reclaims first, or the declared server-wall release race precedes delivery, the server may not yet know the refusal: an unconstrained claim can start a fresh conversation. This is a named residual, not a global guarantee for an unapplied observation. State it in owner docs and A1 and test it explicitly; do not infer historical authority to conceal it.

**Attempt-path decision and disclosure:** main at 95bc98be sets attemptPaths from Docker wiring and drops session_id before transcript checks on every new effective-attempt-path claim (runner.ts:3034, 11661, 11929-11952). Retained recovery can enable that mode on an unwired worker too. The compact feature does not make that path session-compatible. Its Resume action must say **fresh conversation from the saved work**, show preservation provenance, and require explicit owner consent bound to this exact held episode. Once that owner transaction queues the run, the episode is resolved and later claims behave as today, including normal attempt-path session resets. They do not repeatedly re-hold merely because this earlier refusal occurred. A new actual refusal creates a new held episode requiring new consent. This deliberately relaxes until-terminal consent only for the attempt-path rule; do not promise same-conversation resume or blame the provider for a path-based refusal. In-process approval is not itself proof of a new claim. Same-conversation resume on Docker-wired lanes is a separately tracked follow-up, not a blocker here.

**Compatible-to-attempt transition:** a run may first refuse on a compatible path, successfully Preserve, and later be claimed on an attempt path. It must first re-hold with the fresh-from-saved-work warning. If the owner confirms that episode, the transaction clears preserve_required and **ends the earlier compatible-lane until-terminal guarantee for the rest of this run**, including later compatible-path claims, until a new refusal starts a new episode. Show this consequence in the confirmation and owner docs, not only in the implementation comments. It is the approved lane-transition consequence, not a reusable one-claim Fresh grant.

**Operational cost:** an indefinite hold retains affinity and custody. Its retained open custody holds count toward the owner's admission limit of 8 under existing accounting and can block new runs; do not confuse a policy hold with an exemption from that limit. A paused assigned run also keeps the hosted worker busy in ListHostedWorkersForController, so #3103's drain-until-idle/template roll may wait indefinitely. This feature adds no admission relief, force roll or hold timeout; it must not advertise an operationally idle held worker or release custody just to escape the limit. Exercise these consequences in tests and document them.

Owner actions:

- **Resume on an effective-attempt-path lane:** explicitly confirm fresh conversation from the saved work for this refusal episode, with optional guidance. Atomically resolve that episode and queue the run. Later claims follow today's behavior, until another refusal; an old request cannot consent to that later episode. There is no separate per-claim Fresh grant on this lane.
- **Resume on a session-compatible lane, optionally with guidance:** continue the preserved conversation. Missing/unknown transcript or provider rejection re-holds as resume_rejected without thread/start; preserving remains required on later claims. If recovery actually changes to an incompatible attempt path, first re-hold with the newly verified fresh-from-saved-work mode; never silently reinterpret an earlier Preserve action as that consent.
- **Explicit Fresh on a session-compatible lane, subject to its safe-cut checkpoint:** consent permits one fresh conversation under one claim, retaining the until-terminal constraint for later claims. Files, plan approval/framing and ordinary execution constraints remain. A lost response, worker crash, later claim or credential change cannot reuse that permission. If this narrow checkpoint cannot pass, leave this option unavailable and use the scope-cut/partial-delivery decision; do not build a broader grant subsystem. This optional path is distinct from the attempt-path episode Resume.
- **Cancel:** existing terminal cancellation; source/custody disposition remains independently guarded.
- **Existing Stop, conditional:** finalize the existing completed scope only. It is unavailable during a pending revision or when current evidence is uncertain. It must have service-side enforcement and an ordinary-executor zero-new-root-turn proof. If that cannot be achieved narrowly, ship this hold with Stop unavailable, record why, and leave the extension to a follow-up. Do not rebuild the old accepted-Stop subsystem.

Acceptance examples:

1. On a session-compatible lane, an implementation root refuses. The worker captures through its genuine safety boundary and holds, without completion, a second root turn or an MR claiming success. The owner adds guidance and resumes. If the provider supports continuing that session, its next turn includes the guidance. If it refuses again, the run holds again. Simulated provider tests prove both branches; real-provider behavior is A1.
2. On an attempt-path lane, the first refusal holds. A legacy/empty Resume or a token change cannot silently start a fresh conversation. The owner explicitly confirms fresh from saved work; the queued run and a later ordinary pickup need no repeated consent for that resolved episode. A new refusal holds again, and replaying the old episode's confirmation is refused. On a session-compatible lane with a missing conversation, Resume re-holds; only a new explicit one-claim Fresh choice permits a fresh start, and a later reclaim cannot reuse it.
3. A hold POST applies but its response is lost. The owner resumes before local replay. Reconciliation recognizes the already-applied outcome and cannot undo the owner action, reopen a resolved attempt-path episode or consume a compatible-lane Fresh permission. An unapplied surviving refusal can be re-held by the independently authorized current claim without a model turn or consent consumption; absent that authority it stays visibly pending. Cancel remains effective. Stop is offered only under its narrow service-side eligibility.

## Out of scope

- The former D15-D19 accepted-Stop/revision override, policy_stop_* tables/columns, frozen accepted-snapshot SQL encoder, source/feedback receipts, execution projections or a parallel finalization dispatcher.
- Generation-wide Fresh grant arithmetic or a general consent-history subsystem. Attempt-path Resume consumes the held episode directly; only the optional compatible-lane Fresh action uses a pending one-claim intent. Neither is consent for a later refusal.
- Same-conversation resume across Docker/retained-recovery attempt paths. File the named follow-up; do not remove cwd/session or source-exclusion guards in this compact PR.
- Bespoke Slack/TUI action flows. Existing generic displays and controls must remain truthful and cannot bypass consent; dedicated UX is a follow-up. Basic CLI and web owner actions are in scope.
- Late wall-race historical authentication or arbitrary old-generation conversion. Only the narrowly current-owner-authorized re-hold in module 3 is included. Unresolved evidence may block; it may not authorize execution or overwrite a newer owner episode.
- Global exactly-once execution, a shared refusal-intent service, new per-claim journals for ordinary claims, cross-worker custody redesign, quotas or automatic source cleanup.
- Changing provider controls, deliberately provoking a real refusal, changing harness, changing capture/discovery bounds or ADR-2149.
- Workflow edits or workflow-writing validation fixtures, deployment, release/tag creation, worker/PVC cleanup, or any cancellation/discard of the preserved #3068 runs.

### 2. Lane-specific consent: episode Resume versus preserving continuation

The authenticated hold report records a closed continuation_mode: **same_conversation** when effectiveAttemptPaths is false, or **fresh_from_saved_work** when it is true. Use the actual flight mode, not Docker capability alone: retained recovery can enable attempt paths on an unwired lane. Store it with the current applied outcome/episode; expose it in the owner DTO so the UI cannot promise Preserve on an incompatible path. Only a verified current-claim report can set/change it. The episode identity is the outcome id of the current applied hold, including a newly admitted resume_rejected hold; a re-hold is not a new actual provider refusal count.

Keep row-local preserve_required and pending_fresh booleans (default false for existing rows), together with current hold identity/mode and the bounded applied-outcome facts already needed for reconciliation. An unresolved applied hold sets preserve_required. For same_conversation mode that constraint remains until terminal, including after a successful preserving resume or one-claim Fresh start, unless the owner later explicitly resolves an attempt-path episode as disclosed above. It is not inferred merely from the current hold_reason, which clears on continuation.

**Attempt-path episode Resume:** owner Resume is explicitly presented as fresh conversation from saved work. Use the existing Resume route with `mode=fresh_from_saved_work` and expected_episode_id matching the exact current applied codex_policy_hold. In one owner/run transaction, reject stale/foreign/mode-mismatched requests, persist the consent and guidance, clear the old active session_id while retaining it in audit, resolve that episode, clear preserve_required and pending_fresh for this attempt-path episode, and queue the run. This also ends any earlier compatible-lane until-terminal constraint for the remaining run until a new refusal; the owner warning/docs must say so. It is one single-use **episode transition**, not a reusable Fresh permission in ClaimRun. Once accepted, subsequent claims follow today's behavior until a new refusal. Even a crash before thread start or session persistence does not reopen the resolved episode: a later ordinary pickup is baseline behavior under the owner's chosen relaxation, not reuse of a claim grant. Do not impose reconfirmation solely for that resolved episode.

Old consent requests cannot clear a later hold: expected_episode_id and the locked paused/status/owner predicate must still match when the transaction commits. A duplicate response/ack loss can acknowledge only the already-recorded same action or return a safe conflict; it cannot requeue or clear a newer episode. A new actual refusal mints a new immutable outcome/episode, holds again and requires new owner consent. A token change, old audit or an empty/legacy request supplies no such consent. Stop/Cancel remain separate owner dispositions.

**Session-compatible preserving Resume:** mode=preserve, with guidance and exact expected_episode_id, leaves session_id and preserve_required intact and queues under the existing owner transaction. Carry the preserving fact through the neutral turn request to the **single authoritative fresh-thread branch** in CodexHarness, including CodexResumeError fallback. Missing/unknown session or provider rejection re-holds without thread/start. Successful continuation does not clear this lane's until-terminal constraint. If the actual resumed claim now uses an attempt path, re-hold first as fresh_from_saved_work with truthful provenance; do not silently reinterpret the previous Preserve consent as the attempt-lane relaxation.

**Optional compatible-lane Fresh:** the distinct `mode=fresh_one_claim` choice on a same_conversation episode clears the old active session_id (keep it in audit), preserves preserve_required, and sets one pending_fresh intent under the owner transaction. Consume it in **ClaimRun's existing locked-candidate -> UPDATE/RETURNING path**: a materialized locked candidate carries the pre-update pending flag, the successful claim UPDATE clears it, and RETURNING projects that value only for the row actually claimed with its worker/generation. A reclaim cannot reconstruct it from later ACKs, audit or session state. Within the authorized claim, repeated root recreation cannot spend it for another new conversation after one has been established. Do not add this grant mechanism to the attempt-path episode Resume.

For compatible-lane Fresh only, audit consumption before exposing the claim and confirm new-session establishment through the existing current-generation session report. A crash after consumption but before confirmation is shown as **Fresh authorization consumed; no new conversation confirmed**; a later preserving-only claim requires a new explicit choice if its session is unusable. Distinguish crashes before consumption, after consumption/before start, and after start/before identity persistence. If this optional narrow claim checkpoint fails, leave compatible-lane Fresh unavailable via the approved scope-cut rule rather than building generation-wide arithmetic. The required attempt-path episode Resume remains a separate atomic owner transition and is not cut merely because this optional grant failed.

Existing approval/selection framing and earlier owner follow-ups travel into every fresh conversation unchanged (#3149). Neither mode approves a pending plan or changes scope/source/custody authority. The gated plan must map each fact and transaction boundary before implementation and return for review if the actual claim path cannot support the narrow representation. There is no generic consent ledger or second execution-authority protocol.

Offline guard anchors: CodexHarness.startTurn's `this.threadId === undefined` branch calling startThread, not a new parallel dispatcher; the CodexResumeError catch in codex-executor.ts which currently emits continuing-WITHOUT-context before retrying startTurn; and runner.phaseResume's cwd_changed_attempt_path/missing-transcript notices. Move those lineage-break notices after actual fresh admission for a constrained run. A rejected guard reports re-hold, never a fresh session that did not start. Do not change unconstrained behavior or attempt-path safety to make a positive test pass.

### 3. Durable pending refusal and claim-entry reconciliation

Use the authenticated existing outbox/registry and awaited boot/claim-entry reconciliation seams, specifically worker.resolveBootTerminals and the per-run pre-execution resolver boundary. Persist one immutable policy outcome before relinquishing active ownership; bind it to the run, original claim generation and a canonical nonzero UUID outcome id. Do not add a second journal subsystem. Reports and retries reuse that identity, including metadata-free resume_rejected. RetireFinalizeRecord and guarded inventory settlement obey their actual acknowledged disposition and existing cleanup proof.

A surviving unacknowledged outcome blocks provider work before phaseResume. Report application and the bounded last-applied identity commit together under worker/user/claim and run ownership checks. Store the applying worker, applying generation, original source generation and outcome id together. Keep these reconciliation facts when an owner resolves an attempt-path episode; clearing its consent barrier must not erase evidence that prevents replay. One claim can end in at most one admitted immutable policy outcome: a conflicting identity for the same applying generation is refused. Validate authentication and exact recorded/current ownership before using an id for reconciliation; UUID possession alone grants nothing. A matching replay after owner Resume is a no-op, not another pause, counter update, guidance rewrite, compatible-lane Fresh consumption or reopening of a resolved attempt-path episode. A demonstrably newer applied policy episode cannot be rolled back by an older record.

The dispositions are exhaustive for delivery-block retirement (source/custody retirement is separate):

| Evidence and authority | Action |
|---|---|
| This exact outcome applied, or typed already-applied evidence for it | Settle its delivery blocker; retain source/audit artifacts under their existing rules. Do not replay the state mutation. |
| A positively recorded newer applied policy episode (applying generation greater than this record's original source generation) supersedes it | Settle the older delivery blocker with typed resolved-newer evidence; leave the newer episode and owner choices unchanged. Compare the applying generation, not a converted record's older source generation. |
| Outcome is unapplied, and the worker now independently owns an authorized current claim for this run | Before model work or consent use, submit a metadata-free resume_rejected re-hold at the current generation, linked to the original immutable id/source generation and the actual current continuation_mode. It sets the episode barrier, keeps original refusal metadata/count unchanged and consumes no owner consent. Only its acknowledged application settles the original delivery blocker. Retain the original immutable record/artifacts for ordinary retention; do not mutate it into a different request. |
| Outcome is stale/unapplied with no reachable current or recorded authority, or its disposition is uncertain | Keep execution blocked and the original record/source retained, show pending delivery and allow existing Cancel. A generic owner Resume does not clear it. No fabricated terminal disposition or unacknowledged conversion. |
| Authoritatively confirmed terminal/Cancel disposition for this run/outcome | Settle delivery only; preserve independent source/custody checks. |

The current-owner conversion is a new authenticated envelope over unchanged original local evidence, not authorization for an expired G worker to mutate somebody else's G+1 claim. Under the current run lock, recheck worker/user/current positive generation, original identity and whether a newer outcome already applied. The re-hold applies only to the current worker's claimed/running run (or the separately proved revising status). Both forms of owner fresh consent originate only from an exact confirmed applied episode; therefore old replay first resolves that applied/newer episode and cannot re-hold it, reopen its closed barrier or consume its pending/issued compatible-lane Fresh choice. A conversion is not allowed over such a newer owner decision. The resolver runs before model work; uncertainty blocks execution. The consumed-but-unconfirmed claim audit applies only to compatible-lane one-claim Fresh. Reconciliation itself never consumes or remints either form of consent.

This narrow no-model current-owner re-hold closes the stale-record deadlock identified in architecture review. It does not implement arbitrary historical conversion, the old late-wall authenticator or a general replay service. A failed conversion remains blocked; only a positive acknowledged disposition retires its delivery blocker.

Local journal failure/ENOSPC must not remove the flight from snapshots or fall through to a new turn. Replay/backoff is bounded per attempt; boot gate and background drain share the existing singleflight/serialization ownership. Corrupt or over-cap records block rather than being treated as absent. Terminal confirmation or Cancel is reporting evidence, not proof to delete the retained source.

Inherited limitations remain explicit: after a worker and its local journal become unavailable beyond the existing terminal-pending lease (default one hour), another worker cannot consult that journal; the existing server-wall release race can also precede delivery. No global exactly-once or retroactive prevention of an already claimed unconstrained turn is promised. Do not widen those limits, hide them as successful hold delivery, or add global claim footprints to eliminate them.

Extend the existing provider-hold path only for an owner-held noninteractive milestone issue with at least one completed milestone, current approved/executable scope, and no pending revision, candidate approval, unresolved feedback or uncertain gate state.
