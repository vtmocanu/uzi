## Problem and outcome

Settled attempt trees fill Docker workers' data volumes (#3329). At a verified eligible epoch under EX, before admission, reclaim bounded, freshly authorized sources through the existing discard core (ledger `retired`); keep all other sources. Extend the shadow evaluation narrowly for strict plain-journal/live-ledger settlement instead of restricting candidates to PRD A's old would-delete list. Separate physical deletion of present trees from metadata-only settlement of missing trees.

The epoch proof covers the immediate predecessor, intervening pods and concurrent workers. It does not exclude every historical writer: an older pod's process may survive its termination and still write after a later evidenced epoch. The owner accepted that residual on 2026-10-24. Keep stopped containers and existing exit-137 refusals; no new residual is inferred from missing evidence.

Acceptance examples: eligible settled sources are removed and retired while held/shared sources remain; a settled permission refusal keeps the source and yields a partial pass; unknown destructive work prevents admission and exits non-zero with the one-shot consumed. A missing tree is not counted as physical reclaim or proof of authority.

## Shared discard and strict-plain settlement

- Extend `discardRetainedSource` in `agent/src/git.ts` with a typed, worker-owned exclusion-proof argument for the narrow automatic path; do not add a separate eligibility module or parallel disposal implementation. Reuse `evaluateRetained`, inspect confirmation, source-use guards and existing authority/run/bare lock order. A lane's proof must be established by its trusted caller, not model input. Startup epoch proof never authorizes in-life deletion.
- Freshly require positive terminal lifecycle for every source kind and a known exact-run server response positively establishing zero open holds. A 404, unreadable custody, absent record, timer or disk pressure grants no authority. Serialize against source adoption/consumers and recheck pod-entry evidence before each source. Keep all other disposition/refusal guards.
- A strict plain journal requires a **new exact-key-set check**: exactly `runId`, `clonePath`, `attemptId`, valid and matching the exact attributed attempt. Existing `journalState: plain` is insufficient. Under the bare lock compare the raw journal unchanged; do not rely on the shared inspect digest to cover journal contents and do not change that digest's shared contract.
- With full authority and exact writer exclusion, waive only `ledger_live` for that attempt and `named_by_journal` for that exact plain journal. Another record naming the source/path, a successor, transfer, retained source or any other consumer keeps it. Do not waive a category of journal blockers or use terminal status as source-consumer exclusion.
- Any extra journal field, including `restoreTip`, `retainedSources`, `recovery`, `completionSourceFence` or `peeledMarkers`, refuses unchanged. FINAL-, hold- and episode-bound records keep their existing owner disposition paths. This releases no hold and authorizes no other source.
- Present sources use in-place `discardRetainedSource` with the existing inspect `confirm` and a reclaim `quiesce` combining process proof and epoch verdict. Never call `teardownDocker` or `withRetainedSourceBoundary`, which can delete containers. Clear only the unchanged exact journal and append `retired` once disposal is settled. Missing sources need the same full authority for metadata-only settlement.
- Preserve recoverable metadata on retained/uncertain outcomes. Interrupted settlement must remain discoverable after tree removal and either metadata write; the gatherer currently skips retired ledger entries. At the plan gate choose the smallest existing-state recovery sequence that finishes only the exact authorized settlement, never a successor's journal. Reversing write order alone is not a proof. No new API, migration or ledger state without a concrete gap returned to review.

## Lock lifetime, settlement and bounds

- Every mutating child receives the reclaim lock descriptor until it ends, including `runRunnerUidDelete` and git children mutating refs/worktrees/records. Scope one spawn-option hook to the discard core's async context. Run/SDK spawns never inherit the descriptor, whose open-file-description owner could unlock it. Cite any excluded child's inability to mutate protected state.
- Classify settlement from per-source outcomes and actual pending-child/latch state, **not** `kind: incomplete` alone. An incomplete result containing only settled `retained`/`removed`/`missing` outcomes keeps required metadata and may continue. An unknown outcome, unresolved child, latch `stopped()`, `RunnerDeleteUnsettledError` or nonempty `unsettledDeletes` prevents lock release/conversion and admission.
- After all work settles, close EX and acquire SH boundedly with admission still closed; admit only after SH succeeds. On shutdown/deadline start no new source and join in-flight work within PRD A's total/settlement bounds, otherwise exit non-zero. Never release exclusion to meet a deadline.
- Retain bounded inventory/pass caps; M1a measures their widened scope before M1b. Report `would_delete` and `would_settle` separately with present/missing classification, then actual deleted versus metadata-settled counts and bytes as `measured`, `partial` or `unknown`. No bytes-freed claim for a missing source.

## #3775 partial-shadow diagnostic contract

`runPodEpochShadow` adds `partial_causes` to its result; `main.ts` spreads that result into the `pod_epoch_reclaim` log. The fixed vocabulary is defined in `agent/src/pod-epoch-partial-causes.ts`. The `partial_causes` object is diagnostic-only: it changes no candidate, verdict, reason, authority, cap, retry policy or deletion/settlement permission. (The same log line now also carries the M1b reclaim counters recorded in the 2026-11-06 delivery section; the log as a whole is a report and is never read back as authority.) The operator retained-source server explicitly projects the existing inspection fields; this adds no CLI/API response field.

Counters are sparse positive integer counts of observed events, with zero-valued keys omitted and each key saturating at 65535. The unit is a cap encounter, refusal or failure event, not bytes, affected paths, sources or an exact overflow size. Bounded/aborted work makes these observed counts lower bounds; independent causes coexist. The object contains fixed keys and counts, without raw errors, URLs, paths or IDs.

The complete counter family follows. Each prefix/suffix combination in a row is a separate key; standalone rows list full keys.

Helper details travel through error wrappers and are committed at inventory catches, without counting the carried error again. Original-site specifics take precedence over generic catch explanations; aggregation uses the unclassified fallback only when merging supplied details finds no valid positive counter. A swallowed error that becomes only a candidate blocker must not introduce `inventory_partial` or counters on an otherwise complete pass; the one exception is the localized tree-local measurement shortfall (#3851, below), whose counters stay on an otherwise eligible pass. Fresh per-call async-local error sidecars isolate concurrent epoch inspections/operator calls on the same `GitCache` and separate shadow passes.

Measurement details merge in each executed walk's callback, including its `finally` path. Repeated uncached walks of the same path each contribute their own read/close failures: two thrown walks with one read failure and one close failure apiece yield `measurement_filesystem_error: 4`. Genuine measurement cache hits bypass the walk and add no counts. This event accounting is separate from physical-path candidate deduplication.

Existing guard precedence is preserved: group count before group physical paths; inspection descriptors before inspection physical paths; measurement depth before pending-stack capacity. Census generic failure classification checks signal interruption before deadline, then unreadable. Inspection interruption classification checks signal, observed inspection budget, absolute deadline, then generic interruption, while a carried helper detail retains its original cause.

`readFailureCause` follows at most four error/cause objects, stops cycles and guards property getters. It inspects numeric structured `status` and at most 128 characters each of `name`/`code`, without inspecting message, request or body. The first valid integer status from 100 through 599 wins (including a later cause's status over an earlier timeout/transport indication); otherwise timeout precedes transport, then other. Classification emits the fixed family key, not the inspected values.

A structured `TimeoutError` name or recognized timeout code yields `ownership_read_timeout` (or `holds_read_timeout` for holds), unless a structured HTTP status takes precedence. An untyped pre-request ownership budget-deadline failure from `getRunOwnership` is `ownership_read_other` when it reaches inventory classification: the local budget expired before the request, which does not establish that an HTTP call timed out. Classification does not match error messages or change exception types or catch semantics. Aborting the whole pass retains its existing reasons, including `pass_deadline` and `shutdown`.

#3851 localized measurement shortfall (2026-11-10): epoch inspection (the pod-epoch reclaim shadow pass) records a typed `measurementShortfall` (`eacces`, `eperm`, `entry_cap`, `depth_cap`, `pending_cap`) on the affected tree's part and leaves its bytes unmeasured (null); such a shortfall no longer makes the whole pass `inventory_partial`. Tree-local means EACCES/EPERM from opendir of the tree root or any directory below it, EACCES/EPERM from lstat of an entry below the root, or the measurement entry, depth or pending-directory cap. These stay pass-wide: a failure lstat-ing the root itself, a directory read or close failure (any errno), any other errno (EIO, EMFILE, ...), measurement deadline, an expired inspection budget or absolute deadline, cancellation, binding failure, global census/metadata/path/descriptor/group caps, ownership/holds read failures other than the definitive exact ownership 404, authority failures and post-effect uncertainty. A budget, deadline or abort that has already passed outranks a local stop; within one tree the walk stops at the first stop, so the first observed stop wins. The shortfall tree stays ineligible because the present-candidate rule requires `bytesState === "measured"`, and a descriptor with any byte-incomplete part yields no candidate for any of its parts, missing parts included. Independently eligible siblings (another selector of the same run, or another run) are no longer blocked; run-scoped local custody records (recovery, settlement or terminal protection, read per run) still block every selector of that run, while a record-scoped blocker (including the part-level `live:`, `unsafe_path:` and `named_by_*:` blockers; the strict-plain basis discounts only the descriptor's own `ledger_live` and `named_by_journal`) refuses the whole descriptor (source) it belongs to, every part included. Consumers alone refuse nothing. A physical path refused in one descriptor stays refused pass-wide, and the existing run- and worker-wide blockers (worker quarantine, shared or unreadable owed pins) are unchanged; otherwise no other descriptor is affected. `partial_causes` counters such as `measurement_filesystem_error` and `measurement_entry_cap` can therefore appear on an `eligible` verdict and `bytes_state` can be `partial` (or `unknown`, for example when every examined tree falls short; unsafe parts already left bytes unmeasured before #3851, which adds one more cause); they stay diagnostic and grant no eligibility, authority or deletion permission. At the deletion-phase re-inspection a present source is refused unless its fresh `bytesState` is `measured`. Downstream, #4043 and #4007 must not read `eligible` as "all measured", and #4007 must require every part of a disposed descriptor to be measured. Operator (retained-source) inspection is unchanged. Deletion is off by default (the 2026-11-10 #4134 row replaced the compile-time constant with a startup opt-in); this change does not activate it, and live enablement must use an image containing this change and refreshed R9 measurements.
